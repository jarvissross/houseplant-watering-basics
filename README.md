# Houseplant Watering Basics: A Free Beginner's Guide

Most houseplants that die, die from watering mistakes. This short guide gives you a simple method you can use with any plant and any pot, without a schedule you have to memorize.

## 1. The one rule
A fixed "water every Sunday" schedule fails because the soil dries at different speeds depending on pot size, light, room temperature and plant type. Instead, check first, then water only if the check says yes. A plant that is a little too dry usually recovers. A plant kept constantly wet slowly rots at the roots.

## 2. Three ways to check if a plant is thirsty
**Finger test.** Push a finger into the soil up to your first knuckle (about an inch, 2.5 cm). Damp or cool means wait. Dry and crumbly means water.

**Lift test.** Lift the pot right after a thorough watering and remember how heavy it feels. Lift it again every few days. A pot that feels much lighter is dry. This works well for pots you can pick up with one hand.

**Wooden stick test.** Push a plain wooden skewer or chopstick to the bottom of the pot and leave it for a minute. If it comes out clean and dry, the whole pot is dry. If dark soil clings to it, wait.

Which plants like which? As a loose guide: succulents and cacti want the soil to dry out completely between waterings. Many common leafy plants like the top inch or two to dry. Ferns and some tropical plants prefer soil that stays slightly damp but never soggy. When you buy a plant, look up that specific plant's preference and write it on the planner below.

## 3. How to water properly
- Use room-temperature water. Water straight from the tap is fine for most plants; if a plant's leaf tips go brown over time, try letting a jug of water stand overnight or use collected rainwater.
- Water slowly and evenly over the whole soil surface, not just one spot.
- Keep going until water runs out of the drainage holes at the bottom. This wets all the roots and flushes out built-up minerals.
- Let the pot drain for 10 to 15 minutes, then empty the saucer. A plant standing in a full saucer keeps soaking up water and the roots stay wet.
- If the soil has become very dry and shrunk away from the pot's edge, water runs straight down the gap. In that case, set the pot in a basin with a few inches of water for 20 to 30 minutes ("bottom watering") so the soil can soak it up, then drain.

## 4. Pots, drainage and saucers
Drainage holes are the most important feature of any pot. If you love a decorative pot that has no hole, use it as a cover: keep the plant in a plain plastic pot with holes and set that inside the decorative one. Take the inner pot out to water it, let it drain, and put it back.

Unglazed clay pots dry faster because water escapes through the clay. Plastic and glazed pots hold water longer. Choosing the right pot for the plant is a quiet way to make watering easier. A small plant in a very big pot stays wet too long because there are not enough roots to use the water.

## 5. Reading the leaves
Leaves give hints, but several problems look alike, so use them together with the soil check.

| What you see | Often points to | What to try |
|---|---|---|
| Drooping, soil dry and light pot | Too dry | Water thoroughly |
| Drooping, soil wet | Too wet, roots struggling | Stop watering, let soil dry, check drainage |
| Yellow lower leaves, soil always damp | Too much water | Water less often, improve drainage |
| Crispy brown tips | Dry air, uneven watering, or minerals | Water evenly, try standing water |
| Brown, soft, dark patches | Often too wet | Let it dry; trim soft parts with clean scissors |
| Leaves curling inward | Dry soil or hot spot | Check soil, move away from heaters |

If a plant keeps declining after you fix watering, look at light next. Too little light means a plant uses water slowly, so the same watering that was fine in summer is too much in a dim room.

## 6. Seasons change the answer
Most plants grow less in the short days of winter, so they use less water. Check the soil as usual, but expect longer gaps between waterings from late autumn to early spring. In a bright, warm summer room you may water twice as often. Keep plants away from heaters, vents and cold drafts, which dry or chill them quickly.

## 7. A weekly five-minute routine
Pick one day each week as "plant check day". Do not water everything; just check everything.
1. Do the finger test on each pot and write down the result.
2. Water only the dry ones, the full way described above.
3. Empty every saucer.
4. Turn each plant a quarter turn so it grows evenly toward the light.
5. Pick off any dead leaves, and wipe dusty leaves with a damp cloth so they can catch light.

## 8. Checklist
- [ ] Every pot has drainage holes (or sits as an inner pot inside a cover pot)
- [ ] I checked the soil before watering, not the calendar
- [ ] I watered until it ran out the bottom, then emptied the saucer
- [ ] I know whether each plant likes to dry out fully or stay lightly damp
- [ ] I reduced watering in the dim months
- [ ] I picked a weekly check day
